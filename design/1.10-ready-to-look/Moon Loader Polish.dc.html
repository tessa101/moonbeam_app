Same size, position and 4.8 s cycle. The direction is reversed: it now waxes from the right, the way the moon does in the northern sky.

Polished spec
Shape. The lit side is a semicircle plus a half-ellipse with x-radius r·|cos θ|, where θ runs 0→2π over the cycle. It's straight at the quarters, a crescent before them and gibbous after.
Direction. It waxes on the right (θ 0→π), then wanes on the left (π→2π).
Dark side. #2A2127 (earthshine), so new moon reads as a dim moon, not a hole.
Halo. It follows the lit fraction k = (1 − cos θ)/2: opacity 0.12 + 0.88k, scale 0.94 + 0.1k. The tight glow is 0.35k. There's no separate clock, so it's brightest at full and nearly gone at new.
Timing. 4.8 s per cycle: 2.2 s waxing, a 0.4 s hold at full, then 2.2 s waning. Each sweep eases in and out with θ = π·(q − 0.85·sin(2πq)/2π), where q runs 0 → 1 over that sweep, so it speeds up from a standstill and slows to a stop.
Edge. A 0.8 pt Gaussian blur on the lit shape, clipped to the disc.
Stop. It runs forward at 2.6× speed to the hold phase (waxing gibbous, about 85% lit, the mirror of today's hold), freezes, then the halo pulses at ±30% over 1.8 s.
