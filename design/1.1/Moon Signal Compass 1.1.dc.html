Dial
Ticks every 2°, longer every 10°, and heavy every 30°. Numbers sit at each 30° step between the cardinals, so you always have a reference within 15°.
The needle starts right under the readout and runs into the tick ring, so the readout and the dial read as one thing. On lock, the needle and readout both turn amber.
The crosshair stays fixed and doesn't rotate.
Now vs tonight
If the moon is up, the prompt says "now" and the moon glyph pulses on the arc. The pulse stops the first time the user locks on any target, and it doesn't come back for that session.
If the moon is down, the prompt says "tonight" and there's no glyph on the dial.
The prompt is always exactly three lines: "Where can I find" / "the moon [when]" / "in [city]?". A long city name gets an ellipsis on line 3, so the layout below never moves.
Arc
The arc shows the moon's path from rise to set, outside the ring. The part it has already travelled is a solid line, and the part still to come is dotted. When the moon is down, the whole of tonight's path is dotted and dimmer.
The labels are "↑ Rise", "↓ Set" and "Now". The locked target drops its label, because the readout pill says the same thing.
Table
On lock, the matching cell gets an amber outline and its label turns amber.
Your question: a third row, "Up now", shows the moon's live bearing and where it is between last rise and next set. Locking on the moon highlights this row. When the moon is down, the row says when it rises next, so the table stays the same height.
